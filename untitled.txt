To find the best number of clusters, I looked at both the Elbow and Silhouette plots together. Because keeping 95% variance left us with 12 dimensions, the graphs don't look perfectly textbook-clean—which is normal when data is spread out across so many features. However, K = 5 stands out as the best choice. The Silhouette graph shows a clear local peak at 5, meaning the groups are tightly formed there, and the Elbow graph shows a slight bend right at that same spot. Following the final project step, I trained the K-Means model using all 12 dimensions but plotted the results using just the first two principal components so we can easily visualize the patient groups on a 2D graph
